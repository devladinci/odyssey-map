# Statements that need a second look

The texts were written from knowledge of the poem and of the literature, and then checked where a check was possible (see the end of this file). The statements below could not be checked against a source while writing. Each needs a look at the poem or the literature, and then either a fix in both languages or removal from this list.

## People and days

- `days.json`: the split of the poem into forty-one days. The evening in Eumaeus' hut (Book 15, lines 301–494) is put on the day Telemachus sails from Pylos; some count it a day earlier. Some scholars count forty days in all.
- `people.json`: Megapenthes is listed as a son of Menelaus without a note on his mother in the tree itself (the note says "by a slave woman"). The tree "The sons of Poseidon" joins three different mothers.
- `heroes.json`: Eurylochus is called "a kinsman of Odysseus" (Book 10, line 441 says a relative by marriage). The scenes on a character card are found by searching the scene texts for the name as a whole word.

## Scenes

The line where each scene starts and ends was read in the Greek text of the Perseus Digital Library, and the summaries were written from that text and from A. T. Murray's translation. Judgement calls:

- **Boundaries inside a long speech**: 3.3 and 3.4 (lines 130 and 184 of Nestor's speech), 4.5 and 4.7 (lines 351 and 587 of Menelaus' speech), 12.3 (line 73 of Circe's speech).
- **Boundaries in mid-verse**: where an episode begins in the middle of a line, the scene starts on that line: 13.4 (125), 13.5 (187), 15.9 (495), 16.4 (155), 17.4 (182), 21.9 (393).
- **Very short scenes**, kept because they are a journey or a change of place: 6.7, 9.2, 10.2, 12.9, 17.2, 24.1, 24.9.
- **Places listed without being named in the lines**: Olympus in 5.1, Ithaca in 5.1 and 5.4, Troy in 8.2, Mycenae in 11.7, Knossos in 11.10, Asteris in 15.1 and 16.8.
- **Journeys drawn further than the text goes**: 3.9 draws the chariot road as far as Sparta, though Book 3 ends on the road; 5.8 ends at the river mouth one line early.
- **A disputed word**: *lykabas* (14.161, 19.306) is given as "at the change of the moon", following the next line.
- **Left out on purpose**: Homer's own glances ahead (17.364, 18.155–156, 18.345, 20.392–394, 21.98–100, 21.418, 24.470–471), so that no scene gives away a later one.
- **Minor persons left unnamed**, to keep the texts short: among them Pontonous, Castor son of Hylax, Mesaulius, Arnaeus (the real name of Irus), Echetus in Book 18, Eurybates.

## Names

- English uses the familiar Latin forms (Odysseus, Circe, Aeaea, Cythera). The ids keep Greek-style spellings (`kythera`, `sounion`, `geraistos`).
- Bulgarian follows Georgi Batakliev's translation where it was checked (Герайст, Фере, Фестос, Форкин, Елисейските полета), with three deliberate differences: Сама (he has Зама or Самос), тафийци (тафци) and Псира (Псирия). Кирка is used, not Цирцея.
- The city of Athens and the goddess Athena are both „Атина“ in Bulgarian. The texts say „град Атина“ where the two meet.
